Laptop Running Slow? 12 Fixes That Actually Work

By TechSaathi Team · Updated: November 1, 2026 · ~8 min read

A laptop that took 40 seconds to boot last year now takes four minutes. The cursor spins while you switch tabs, YouTube stutters, and you are already browsing "new laptop price". Before you spend money — most slow laptops are not old, they are crowded. Work through these twelve fixes in order; the first six alone usually bring a machine back to life.

1. Cut Down Startup Programs

Every app that quietly starts with Windows eats memory before you even click anything. On Windows, press Ctrl + Shift + Esc to open Task Manager, go to the Startup apps tab, and disable everything you do not need the moment the laptop wakes — updaters, chat apps, music helpers. On macOS, check System Settings → General → Login Items. This single fix is the closest thing to magic for boot time.

2. Check What Is Eating Your RAM Right Now

In Task Manager (or Activity Monitor on Mac), sort running apps by memory. There is almost always one surprise — a browser with 40 tabs, or a game launcher running in the background for weeks. Close the heavy ones you are not using. Browsers are the usual suspect: each tab is a small program of its own.

3. Free Up the System Drive

Windows needs breathing room on the C: drive to function — when it is nearly full, everything from booting to saving files slows down. Aim to keep at least 15–20% of the drive empty. Uninstall programs you never use (Settings → Apps), move your photos and videos to an external drive or cloud, and run Disk Cleanup or Storage Sense to clear old temporary files safely.

4. Update — the OS and the Hidden Apps

Updates are not just security patches; they include performance fixes for bugs that were slowing your machine. Install pending Windows Updates or macOS updates, and update your browser and drivers too — graphics driver updates in particular can noticeably smooth out video and gaming.

5. Restart Properly Once a Week

Modern laptops sleep instead of shutting down, and small memory leaks pile up over weeks of "sleep". A full restart clears the decks. If your laptop has been on for a month, restart it now and notice the difference — it is the oldest fix in the book for a reason.

6. Hunt Down the Heat

Heat is the silent speed killer. When the cooling vents are dusty, the processor deliberately slows itself down to avoid overheating — a state called thermal throttling. If your laptop gets hot and loud before it gets slow: clean the vents with compressed air, always use it on a hard flat surface (never on the bed or pillow), and consider a simple cooling stand for heavy work. A laptop that runs cool runs fast.

7. Check Your Power Plan

Windows laptops often run in a power-saving mode that limits performance to stretch battery life. If you are plugged in anyway, switch to Best Performance (Settings → Power & battery) or set the power mode to Balanced on battery and Performance on charging. This is free speed with one click.

8. Scan for Malware — Once

Cryptominers and adware are built to consume your computer for someone else's benefit. Run a full scan with Windows Security (built-in, free, and honestly good) rather than installing a random "cleaner" app from a video ad. Speaking of which —

9. Delete the "Cleaner" Apps

Ironically, the biggest source of slowdowns on family laptops is the pile of speed-boosting, cleaning, driver-updating apps installed from YouTube ads. They run constantly in the background "helping" you, nag you with pop-ups, and often install more of themselves. Pick one: Windows Security for protection. Uninstall the rest.

10. Browser Slimming

If slowness is mostly while browsing: remove extensions you do not actively use (each one runs on every page), keep tabs under control with a bookmarks folder for "later", and try clearing the browser cache. On 4–8 GB RAM laptops, one browser window with disciplined tabs beats three windows with forty.

11. Reset Windows Without Losing Files

If years of software layers have made things hopeless, Windows has a built-in reset that reinstalls the system while keeping your personal files: Settings → System → Recovery → Reset this PC → Keep my files. Back up first anyway, budget an hour, and a five-year-old installation often feels factory-fresh afterwards.

12. When It Is Genuinely the Hardware

Two upgrades rescue old laptops better than any new purchase: an SSD (if the laptop still uses a spinning hard disk — this is transformative) and extra RAM (8 GB to 16 GB). A service centre can do both for a reasonable price, and together they typically cost a fraction of a new laptop while making an old machine feel years younger.

Conclusion

Slow laptops are usually crowded laptops: too many startup apps, a full disk, dust in the vents, and a few "helper" programs quietly fighting each other. Work through this list top to bottom, and only if everything is clean should you start shopping for hardware. In our experience, eight out of ten "dying" laptops just needed an evening of attention.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much RAM do I actually need in 2026?

8 GB is the workable minimum for browsing and office work; 16 GB is the comfortable standard, especially if you keep many tabs open or edit photos. If your laptop has 4 GB, a RAM upgrade will change your daily experience more than any software tweak.

Does factory resetting delete my photos and documents?

The "Keep my files" reset preserves your personal files but removes installed programs and settings. Still, treat any reset as a backup event first — copy photos, documents and browser bookmarks to a pen drive or cloud before you begin.

My laptop is 7 years old — repair or replace?

If it already has an SSD and 8+ GB RAM and is still too slow, the processor itself is likely the limit, and replacing the laptop makes sense. If it still runs a hard disk, an SSD upgrade first — it is the cheapest possible test of whether the machine has more life in it.

Most "slow" laptops just need a clean-up evening — start with fix #1! 💻
