Phone Overheating? 9 Real Fixes That Actually Work

By TechSaathi Team · Updated: August 29, 2026 · ~6 min read

The phone is too hot to hold after a gaming session, warm when charging, or burning up simply sitting in your pocket on a summer day. Some warmth is normal — phones are small computers working hard in a sealed body — but constant overheating is a warning sign. It slows the processor, drains the battery faster, and over time permanently damages the battery itself. Here's why it happens and how to fix it.

Why Phones Get Hot

  • Heavy workloads — gaming, video calls, and camera use push the processor to its limit
  • Charging — especially fast charging, which naturally produces heat
  • Direct sunlight or hot surroundings — a phone on a car dashboard can cross dangerous temperatures within minutes
  • Runaway background apps — a misbehaving app can keep the processor busy even when the screen is off
  • Faulty or cheap chargers and cables — unstable power delivery means wasted energy as heat

1. Never Game or Stream While Charging

Charging produces heat; gaming produces heat. Together they push the phone past its thermal limits — the worst combination for battery health. If you must play while plugged in, at least remove the case and keep brightness low. Ideally, charge first, play after.

2. Remove the Case When It's Hot

A thick cover works like a jacket — it traps heat inside. When the phone feels warm or you're about to start a long gaming session or charge it, take the case off. It's the simplest cooling trick there is.

3. Give It Shade — Immediately

If the phone has been in direct sunlight and feels hot, move it into shade right away and let it cool before heavy use. Never leave it on a car dashboard, near a window in the afternoon, or under a pillow — a phone covered by a blanket can overheat even while idle.

⚠️ Never cool a hot phone in a fridge or with ice. Sudden temperature changes cause condensation inside the phone — water damage without a drop of water touching it. Let it cool at room temperature.

4. Find the App That's Causing It

Settings → Battery (or Device care → Battery) shows which apps consumed the most power since the last full charge. An app you barely use sitting near the top of that list is suspect — update it, restrict its background activity, or uninstall it. Rogue apps are the most common cause of a phone that heats up while doing nothing.

5. Drop the Brightness and Close the Extras

Full brightness plus a heavy app plus background sync is a recipe for heat. Turn on adaptive brightness, clear recent apps before gaming, and turn off hotspot/Bluetooth/location when you don't need them. Each one removes load from the processor.

6. Use the Original Charger

Cheap chargers and worn-out cables deliver unstable power — part of that energy turns into heat inside both the charger and the phone. Stick to the original charger, or a certified one from the phone's maker. And avoid no-name fast-charging bricks that claim speeds your phone was never designed for.

7. Skip Wireless Charging on Hot Days

Wireless charging runs noticeably warmer than cable charging. On hot days, or when the phone is already warm, use the cable — it's faster and cooler. If your phone sits on a wireless pad all day at work, that constant gentle heat adds up over the battery's life.

8. Keep Software Updated

Both system updates and app updates regularly include fixes for overheating bugs — vendors track thermal issues closely and patch misbehaving components. If your phone started running hot after a specific update, check for the follow-up patch; it usually arrives within weeks.

9. Know When It's Serious

Warm during gaming, charging, or video calls: normal. Hot while idle with the screen off, hot enough to be uncomfortable within minutes of light use, or heat paired with rapid battery drain: not normal. And if the back panel or screen starts lifting, or you see the battery appearing swollen under the cover — stop using the phone and take it to a service center immediately. A swollen battery is a safety hazard.

Conclusion

Heat is the fastest way to age a phone — it throttles performance today and kills the battery over months. Keep it out of the sun, don't stack heavy tasks on top of charging, remove the case when it counts, and evict misbehaving apps. Your phone (and your next battery budget) will thank you.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is it normal for a phone to get warm while charging?

Mildly warm, especially with fast charging — yes, that's normal. Uncomfortably hot to hold, or hot enough that charging slows down — that's not. Try the original charger, remove the case, and if it persists, get the battery checked.

Does heating damage the battery permanently?

Yes — sustained heat above roughly 35-40°C is the fastest known way to permanently reduce lithium battery capacity. Occasional warmth is harmless; constant heat is what shortens battery lifespan.

My phone overheats only in one app. What should I do?

Update the app first — if the heat started after an app update, the developer may have already shipped a fix. If it still overheats, clear the app's cache, and as a last resort reinstall it. Some poorly optimized apps are simply heavy — find a lighter alternative.
