Lost or Stolen Phone? Do These 5 Things Immediately

By TechSaathi Team · Updated: August 29, 2026 · ~6 min read

The phone's not in your pocket. Not in the bag. Not at the last table. That sinking feeling is bad enough — but the real danger isn't the ₹15,000 device, it's what's inside it: your UPI, your banking apps, your email, your WhatsApp, your photos. The first hour matters enormously. Here's the exact order to act — speed matters, so skim the bold parts now and read the rest later.

1. Try to Find It — 10 Minutes, No More

From any browser, open google.com/android/find (Find My Device) and sign in with the Google account that's on the lost phone. If the phone is on and connected, you'll see its location on a map — you can also make it ring at full volume even if it was on silent. Check the sofa cushions via the ring, not by memory. If it shows a location you recognize, great. If not, don't chase it yourself — move to step 2.

iPhone users: iCloud.com/find does the same. If the phone shows "offline", don't lose hope — the moment it connects to any network, the actions below still fire.

2. Lock It and Leave a Message — Right Now

In Find My Device itself, use Secure device: it locks the screen instantly (even if there was no lock) and lets you display a message with an alternate contact number — "Lost phone, please call 98XXX". Honest finders exist. This costs you 30 seconds and should happen before anything else, because it protects everything behind the lock screen.

3. Kill the Money Channels — SIM and UPI

This is the step most people forget in the panic, and it's the one that actually protects your money:

  • Block the SIM — call your operator's customer care from a borrowed phone (Jio 1800-889-9999, Airtel 198, Vi 199 — keep these saved). A blocked SIM can't receive OTPs.
  • Call your bank — tell them the phone is lost and ask them to block mobile/UPI banking on the account.
  • On another device, log out remote sessions — Google Account → Security → Your devices → sign out the lost phone; similarly for Paytm/PhonePe/GPay if accessible.

4. File an FIR With the IMEI

The IMEI is your phone's unique fingerprint — a stolen phone can be blacklisted with it. Dial *#06# on... well, on your other phones right now — write down the IMEIs of every phone in the house today, because after a theft it's too late for the lost one. With the IMEI in hand, file a police complaint (many states allow this online at their cybercrime portals). A theft FIR also helps with insurance claims and often with getting the device blocked carrier-side.

5. Change the Critical Passwords

From another device, change the password of your Google account first (it's the master key — it controls the lost phone, Gmail, Photos, Drive). Then WhatsApp: on your new/temporary phone, register your number on WhatsApp (after the SIM is reissued) — the old device gets kicked out automatically. Then change email and banking passwords. Quick note: our WhatsApp privacy guide shows how to enable two-step verification so a stolen phone can't re-register your WhatsApp even with the SIM.

Only After All That: Erase It Remotely

Once the SIM is blocked, accounts are locked, and the FIR is filed, use Erase device in Find My Device to factory-reset the phone remotely. Do this after the protective steps — if there's any chance of recovering the phone, erasing early doesn't help you, and it also cuts off the find-my connection.

💡 Do this TODAY for future-proofing: (1) note your IMEI via *#06# and save it somewhere that isn't your phone; (2) confirm Find My Device is ON (Settings → Security); (3) enable two-step verification on WhatsApp; (4) make sure your screen lock and a strong Google password are in place. Four checkboxes that turn a panic into a procedure.

Conclusion

The order is: find → lock → SIM + bank → FIR → passwords → erase. Notice that "buy a new phone" isn't even in the first five steps — protecting what's inside the old one matters a hundred times more. And the best time to prepare for a lost phone is today, while it's still in your pocket.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can a thief unlock my phone and access my UPI?

Only if the phone has no screen lock or they watch you enter it before stealing. With a lock screen active, your UPI PIN is a second barrier they don't have. This is why screen lock + a changed Google password + blocked SIM (no OTPs) within the hour makes the theft mostly about the hardware, not your money.

The phone shows "offline" in Find My Device. Is it gone?

Not necessarily — "offline" just means it's switched off or has no connection. Your lock and erase commands queue up and execute the moment it comes online. Meanwhile, proceed with SIM block, bank, and FIR without waiting.

Can police actually recover a stolen phone using the IMEI?

IMEI-based blocking prevents the phone from being used on any network with that IMEI, which kills its resale value and sometimes leads to recovery when the thief tries to use or sell it. Recovery isn't guaranteed, but the FIR + IMEI combo is still the official and most effective route.

Save the helpline numbers and your IMEI today — then share this article so your family does too! 📵
