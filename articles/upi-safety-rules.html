10 UPI Safety Rules That Protect Your Money

By TechSaathi Team · Updated: August 29, 2026 · ~6 min read

UPI itself has never been hacked — it's the system around it that people get caught by: fake QR codes, rushed collect requests, screen-sharing scammers, and the eternal "enter your PIN to receive money". The technology is solid; the habits need work. These 10 rules close the gaps that every UPI fraud relies on.

1. The PIN Rule — Say It Out Loud

A UPI PIN is only ever entered to SEND money. Never to receive it. If any app screen, phone call, or "customer care" person tells you to enter your PIN to get money, you are sending money to them. There is no exception to this rule in the entire UPI system. None.

2. Never Scan a QR Code Someone Sends You to "Receive" Payment

A QR code is a payment address — scanning someone's code and entering your PIN means you are paying them. Sellers at markets and online deal-makers asking you to "scan this QR to receive your payment" are running the classic QR scam. You never scan to receive; you only share your own QR or number to get paid.

3. Read the Payee Name Before Every PIN Entry

The PIN screen always shows the recipient's name. Three seconds of reading prevents the costliest mistakes: paying "Rahul S." instead of "Rahul Saha", or paying a scammer's name while believing you're paying the shopkeeper. If the name looks wrong, cancel — no genuine payee minds.

4. Treat Collect Requests Like Strangers Knocking

A "collect request" is someone asking your permission to pull money from your account. Genuine friends don't need collect requests — they can just request and you pay them yourself. So when a request arrives from an unknown name — especially one claiming to be a bank, a refund, or a cashback — decline it. Verify separately through a channel you trust first.

5. Keep Your Linked Account Light

Don't link the account holding your life savings. Keep a moderate balance in the account connected to UPI — enough for daily use — and keep the bulk of your money in a separate account or deposit. If the worst happens, the damage is capped. This one habit has saved more money than any app setting.

6. Set a Per-Transaction Limit

Every UPI app lets you cap how much a single transaction can be (Settings → payment/bank settings). Set a daily cap you're comfortable losing to a mistake — say ₹5,000-10,000 for everyday use. Even if a scammer tricks you once, the limit stops them from draining the account in one shot.

7. Use UPI Lite for Small Daily Payments

UPI Lite handles small payments (tea, autos, vegetables) from an on-device wallet — no PIN for every payment, and it doesn't even touch your main bank account per transaction. It's faster at crowded counters and adds a layer of separation between daily spending and your actual account.

8. Never Combine UPI With Screen Sharing

No genuine support process involves watching your screen. If someone on a call asks you to install AnyDesk or similar and open your payment app, they intend to watch your PIN and take over your session. End the call. This pattern alone powers a huge share of account-emptying frauds — our scam safety guide covers the full playbook.

9. Act Within Minutes If Something Goes Wrong

Sent money to the wrong number? Called the number politely — if no response, raise it in the app (there's a report/complaint option on the transaction) and inform your bank immediately. Money stolen by fraud? Call 1930 (the national cybercrime helpline) within the first hour — money gets frozen mid-transit more often when reported fast. Also file at cybercrime.gov.in.

10. Reconcile Your Statements Monthly

Once a month, open your bank statement and match the debits with what you remember spending. Small unauthorized deductions (₹99, ₹199 "subscriptions") survive precisely because nobody reads statements. Spot anything unfamiliar → dispute it with the bank that week. Ten minutes, once a month.

Conclusion

Every UPI scam ever run leans on one thing: rushing you past the moment where you'd notice something's wrong. Slow down at the PIN screen, read the payee name, refuse collect requests from strangers, cap your limits, and keep the big money in a different account. UPI's convenience is real — these rules just make sure you keep all of it without the risk.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

I sent money to the wrong UPI ID. Can I get it back?

Sometimes. Immediately raise a complaint in your UPI app against that transaction, then call your bank — they can raise a wrong-credit dispute with the recipient's bank. There's no guarantee (the recipient must consent or be traced), which is exactly why rule 3 — reading the payee name — matters so much.

Are QR codes at shops and petrol pumps safe?

Static stickers at shops have been swapped by scammers with their own codes — the shop loses, you pay a stranger. At a counter, ask the staff to confirm the payee name shown on your screen before entering the PIN. Better yet, scan, then verify the name matches the shop.

Is it safe to save my card or keep auto-pay mandates on UPI apps?

Auto-pay mandates for known services (Netflix, electricity) are generally safe and convenient — review the list yearly and cancel forgotten ones. Be more careful with random small merchants' e-mandates: check any "auto-debit setup" notification carefully before approving.
